## 📜 The 30 Ground-Truth Legal Clauses

### Category 1: Definitions & Basic Scope
1. **Clause 1 (Consumer Definition):** Under Section 2(7), a 'Consumer' is any person who buys goods or hires services for consideration, but excludes anyone who obtains goods for resale or commercial purposes.
2. **Clause 2 (Defect in Goods):** Under Section 2(10), a 'Defect' means any fault, imperfection, or shortcoming in quality, quantity, potency, purity, or standard required by law or contract.
3. **Clause 3 (Deficiency in Service):** Under Section 2(11), a 'Deficiency' means any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in the quality, nature, and manner of performance of a service.
4. **Clause 4 (Unfair Trade Practice):** Under Section 2(47), an 'Unfair Trade Practice' includes false representation of goods, misleading warranty claims, failure to issue bill/cash memo, and refusing to take back defective goods.
5. **Clause 5 (Misleading Advertisement):** Under Section 2(28), misleading advertisements falsely describe products, give false guarantees, or deliberately conceal important information from consumers.

### Category 2: Six Fundamental Consumer Rights (Section 2(9))
6. **Clause 6 (Right to Safety):** Consumers have the right to be protected against marketing of goods, products, and services that are hazardous to life and property.
7. **Clause 7 (Right to Information):** Consumers have the right to be informed about the quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard, and price of goods or services to prevent unfair practices.
8. **Clause 8 (Right to Choice):** Consumers have the right to be assured access to a variety of goods and services at competitive prices wherever possible.
9. **Clause 9 (Right to be Heard):** Consumers have the right to be heard and assured that their interests will receive due consideration in appropriate consumer forums.
10. **Clause 10 (Right to Redressal & Education):** Consumers have the right to seek redressal against unfair trade practices or exploitation, and the right to consumer awareness and education.

### Category 3: Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)
11. **Clause 11 (Establishment of CCPA):** Under Section 10, the Central Government establishes the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) to protect, promote, and enforce consumer rights as a class.
12. **Clause 12 (Recall & Refund Powers):** Under Section 20, the CCPA has the statutory power to order recall of dangerous goods, refund of prices paid, and discontinuation of unfair trade practices.
13. **Clause 13 (Penalties on False Ads):** Under Section 21, the CCPA can impose penalties up to ₹10 Lakhs on manufacturers and endorsers for misleading ads, rising up to ₹50 Lakhs for subsequent violations.
14. **Clause 14 (Ban on Celebrity Endorsers):** The CCPA can prohibit a celebrity endorser of a misleading advertisement from making any endorsement for up to 1 year, and up to 3 years for subsequent violations.

### Category 4: Three-Tier Dispute Redressal Commissions
15. **Clause 15 (District Commission Jurisdiction):** Under Section 34 (as amended), the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission entertains complaints where the value of goods or services paid does not exceed ₹50 Lakhs.
16. **Clause 16 (State Commission Jurisdiction):** Under Section 47 (as amended), the State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission entertains complaints where value exceeds ₹50 Lakhs but does not exceed ₹2 Crores.
17. **Clause 17 (National Commission Jurisdiction):** Under Section 58 (as amended), the National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC) entertains complaints where the value exceeds ₹2 Crores.
18. **Clause 18 (Limitation Period):** Under Section 69, a consumer complaint must be filed within **2 years** from the date on which the cause of action arose.
19. **Clause 19 (Appeals from District Commission):** Under Section 41, any person aggrieved by an order of the District Commission may prefer an appeal to the State Commission within **45 days** from the date of the order.
20. **Clause 20 (Filing Fee Exemption):** Complaints filed in the District Commission with claims up to **₹5 Lakhs** are completely exempt from paying any court or application fee.

### Category 5: Digital Commerce & E-Commerce Rules (2020)
21. **Clause 21 (Grievance Officer Appointment):** Every e-commerce entity must appoint a nodal grievance officer and acknowledge consumer complaints within **48 hours**.
22. **Clause 22 (Complaint Resolution Deadline):** E-commerce entities must resolve consumer grievances within **1 month** (30 days) from the date of receipt of the complaint.
23. **Clause 23 (Mandatory Country of Origin):** E-commerce platforms must clearly display the country of origin, expiry date, manufacturer details, and total price with breakup for all listed goods.
24. **Clause 24 (No Unilateral Cancellation Fee):** E-commerce sellers cannot impose cancellation charges on consumers unless similar charges are borne by the seller for unilateral cancellations.
25. **Clause 25 (Prohibition of Manipulative Flash Sales):** E-commerce entities are prohibited from organizing fraudulent flash sales or back-to-back sales that limit consumer choice or artificially inflate prices.

### Category 6: Product Liability, Mediation & Penalties
26. **Clause 26 (Product Liability Claim):** Under Section 84, a product manufacturer is liable in a product liability action if the product has a manufacturing defect, defective design, or lacks adequate warning instructions.
27. **Clause 27 (Service Provider Liability):** Under Section 85, a product service provider is liable for harm caused if the service provided was deficient, negligent, or violated safety standards.
28. **Clause 28 (Consumer Mediation Cells):** Under Section 74, the State Government establishes Consumer Mediation Cells attached to District and State Commissions to resolve disputes through voluntary mediation.
29. **Clause 29 (Penalty for Non-Compliance):** Under Section 72, failure to comply with an order of the District, State, or National Commission is punishable with imprisonment between **1 month to 3 years**, or fine from ₹25,000 to ₹1 Lakh, or both.
30. **Clause 30 (Frivolous Complaints Penalty):** Under Section 35, where the Commission finds a complaint to be frivolous or vexatious, it may dismiss the complaint and order the complainant to pay costs up to **₹50,000** to the opposite party.
